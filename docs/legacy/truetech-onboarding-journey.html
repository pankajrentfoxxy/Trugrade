Part 1 · Getting in the door

01
Public page · "Sell on TrueTech"

They tell us who they are

Seven fields. Nothing verified yet — the only goal is to capture a real human with a real phone number before they lose interest.

2 minutes No account yet

What we ask

  • Company name * — as they say it, not as it is registered
  • Contact person *
  • Mobile number * — normalised to +91XXXXXXXXXX
  • Work email *
  • City *
  • Laptops sold per month — sets our follow-up priority
  • Brands they deal in — multi-select

What we check silently

  • Duplicate company — fuzzy match against every existing org
  • Blocked list — mobile and email against hashed entries
  • Device velocity — how many signups from this device today
  • Where they came from — campaign attribution, referral code
Clean
Nothing matches. Send the OTP and move to step 2.
Duplicate
A company with a very similar name already exists. We show them: "This company may already be registered — ask your colleague to invite you, or continue if this is a different entity." Sales is notified either way.
Blocked
The mobile or email is on our blocked list. The form appears to submit normally, then the application quietly never progresses. We do not tell a bad actor which of their details we recognised.
Abandoned
They stop here and never come back. The lead is kept with the exact step they dropped at. Sales calls within 24 hours; the product team sees which step loses the most people.

02
OTP screen

We prove the phone and the inbox are theirs

Six digits to the mobile, a link to the email. Both must succeed. This is the cheapest fraud filter we have and it costs a rupee.

1 minute

What we ask

  • Mobile OTP * — 6 digits, valid 5 minutes
  • Email verification * — single-use link
  • Password * — checked against known-breached passwords
  • Two-step login — offered here, compulsory for the owner

How it is protected

  • Codes are hashed — a database leak cannot complete a login
  • Three wrong tries — the code is burned, not just rejected
  • Rate limited — per number and per IP address
  • Purpose-bound — a login OTP cannot sign for a delivery
Both verified
Account and organisation are created. Status becomes REGISTERED. The 7-step tracker appears.
OTP not received
Resend is available after 30 seconds, three times. After three, we offer a voice call. After that, the onboarding desk calls them.
Three wrong codes
The code dies and a fresh one is required. Ten failures in an hour locks the number for 30 minutes with a clear countdown, not a silent failure.
Email bounces
Free-webmail addresses are allowed but flagged for later review. A hard bounce prompts a correction; mobile alone can carry them to step 6.
SMS provider down
Our SMS partner is unreachable. We fail over to the second provider automatically. If both are down, email-only verification is allowed and the mobile is verified later — onboarding is never blocked by our vendor's outage.
Writes identity.otp_request identity.user_account identity.organization kyc.consent_record kyc.onboarding_progress

Step 2 of 7 · Business profile

The registered entity behind the trading name

The type of company decides which documents become compulsory three steps later. Get this wrong and they upload the wrong papers.

3 minutes Draft saved as they type

What we ask

  • Registered legal name *
  • Trading name — what people actually call them
  • Type of company * — Proprietorship, Partnership, LLP, Pvt Ltd, Ltd
  • Date of incorporation
  • Registered address * — PIN code auto-fills city and state
  • Operating address * — often different from registered
  • Business type * — Refurbisher, Dealer, ITAD, Liquidator, OEM partner
  • Website, staff count, year established

What this decides

  • Document checklist — an LLP needs a deed; a proprietorship does not
  • Board resolution requirement — Pvt Ltd and Ltd only
  • PIN code validity — checked against the 19,000-code master
  • Risk signal — a firm registered last month claiming 500 units a month
Saved
The document checklist for step 6 is generated now. They see exactly which papers to keep ready.
Unknown PIN code
The PIN is not in our master list. City and state must be typed manually and the address is flagged for the reviewer.
Very new company
Incorporated within 6 months and claiming high volume. Risk score rises. Approval will need a second reviewer, and possibly a site visit.
They leave mid-form
Half a step is filled. Everything is saved. When they log back in, the form returns exactly as they left it — the header reads "Draft saved 30 seconds ago".

04
Step 3 of 7 · Statutory identity

The government tells us whether they are real

Four live API calls in about eight seconds. This is the step that converts a claim into a fact, and it is where most fraudulent applications die.

2 minutes 4 live checks

What we ask

  • GSTIN * — 15 characters, format checked as they type
  • PAN of the firm *
  • CIN or LLPIN — compulsory for LLP, Pvt Ltd, Ltd
  • Udyam (MSME) number — optional, but it changes how fast we must pay them
  • TAN — only if they deduct tax

What the checks return

  • Registered legal name — this overwrites what they typed. It is the name that will appear on every invoice.
  • Registration status — must read Active
  • State code — decides IGST versus CGST and SGST forever after
  • Taxpayer type — Regular, Composition or SEZ; each is invoiced differently
  • PAN inside the GSTIN — characters 3 to 12 must equal the PAN they entered
Active and matching
Name, state and type are locked in from the government record. Green tick, legal name replaced, straight to step 4.
GSTIN cancelled or suspended
The registration is not active. Hard stop. "This GSTIN is shown as cancelled on the GST portal. Please provide an active registration." They cannot proceed, and no amount of retrying changes it.
PAN does not match the GSTIN
The two documents belong to different entities. Hard stop. In practice this almost always means a borrowed or copied certificate.
Name differs from what they typed
Fuzzy match scores below 85%. We accept the government's version and flag it. The reviewer sees both names side by side.
GST state differs from their address
Registered in Delhi, operating from Noida. Allowed — it is common and legitimate — but recorded, because it affects place of supply.
Third attempt on a different GSTIN
They have now tried three different numbers. Every attempt is logged. Three or more raises the risk score sharply and forces manual review.
Verification service is down
The provider times out or errors. We say so plainly — "We could not reach the GST portal, this is our side, not yours" — save the entry, queue a retry every 15 minutes, and let them continue to step 4. Approval waits for the check; the application does not.
Writes kyc.gst_profile kyc.pan_record kyc.verification_check kyc.tax_declaration platform.integration_log

Step 4 of 7 · Capability

Volume, brands, and where their stock comes from

This is the step no competitor asks for, and it is the one that lets us route a 500-unit request to the three vendors who can actually fill it.

4 minutes

What we ask

  • Categories * — business laptops, workstations, MacBooks, Chromebooks
  • Brands handled *
  • Units per month they can supply *
  • Typical grade mix — e.g. 20% A+, 50% A, 30% B
  • Usual price range
  • Where stock comes from * — corporate buyback, lease return, auction, import, retail exchange
  • Can they give serial numbers before an order? *
  • Do they test in-house? Repair in-house?
  • Lead time in days *

Why each answer matters

  • Capacity — decides which bulk requests reach them
  • Grade mix — a vendor later declaring 100% A+ contradicts their own baseline
  • Sourcing — our first defence against stolen stock, and it decides GST treatment for second-hand goods
  • Serials upfront — decides whether they get serial-first listing or the restricted quantity-only mode
  • In-house testing — these vendors measurably pass our inspection more often
Complete
They are now routable for bulk requests. Continue to facilities.
Capacity looks inflated
Claimed volume is far above what their staff count and warehouse size support. Flagged. The reviewer asks about it, and a site visit may be required before the first payout.
Import or auction sourcing
Higher-risk provenance. Purchase documents become compulsory for every batch above a set value, not optional.
Cannot supply serials upfront
A small dealer without a barcode process. Allowed, but they get quantity-only listing with a 12-hour serial deadline after an order, and a penalty if they miss it. Explained clearly here, not discovered later.

Bike-only access

No warehouse contact mobile

Step 6a of 7 · Documents

The paperwork, checked properly

Six compulsory files, two optional ones that earn a badge on their listings. Every file is encrypted, watermarked when opened, and every single view is logged against a named reviewer.

6 minutes

Required

  • GST registration certificate *
  • PAN card of the firm *
  • Cancelled cheque *
  • Address proof * — dated within 3 months
  • Incorporation document — by company type
  • Authorised signatory ID * — masked Aadhaar only; an unmasked one is rejected
  • Board resolution — Pvt Ltd and Ltd

Optional, earns a trust badge

  • CPCB e-waste authorisation — recycler badge
  • ISO 9001 or R2 certificate — certified-process badge

Checked on every upload

  • Real file type — magic bytes, not the extension. A renamed .exe is caught.
  • Under 5 MB
  • Location data stripped — before it touches our storage
  • File fingerprint — the same document uploaded by two applicants is a document mill
All accepted
Continue to bank details.
Document unreadable or stale
Blurred scan, or an electricity bill from last year. Rejected with the exact sentence they need: "This bill is dated January 2025. Please send one from the last three months." Vague rejections generate support tickets; specific ones get a correct re-upload.
Certificate expires soon
A CPCB authorisation with two months left. Accepted, with an automatic reminder before expiry. When it lapses the badge disappears from their listings on its own — we never display a claim we cannot stand behind.
Unmasked Aadhaar uploaded
Full Aadhaar number visible. Rejected and deleted. We explain why and ask for a masked copy or a different ID. Holding it would be a liability we have no reason to carry.
Same file already used by another applicant
Identical fingerprint on a different organisation. Both applications freeze and go to a fraud reviewer. Neither party is told what triggered it.
Writes kyc.kyc_document vendor.vendor_certification identity.audit_log
08
Step 6b of 7 · Bank account

One rupee, sent to prove the account is theirs

We deposit ₹1 and read back the name the bank holds for that account. It is the most reliable identity check available in India, and it takes about a minute.

2 minutes Live bank check

What we ask

  • Account holder name *
  • Account number * — entered twice, encrypted at rest
  • IFSC * — bank and branch auto-fill
  • Account type * — current, savings, CC or OD

What happens

  • ₹1 credited — the bank returns the registered name
  • Names compared — fuzzy match against the legal name
  • 85% or above — passes automatically
  • 60 to 85% — a person decides
  • Below 60% — fails
Name matches
The account is verified and can receive payouts. Continue to the agreement.
Partial match
"Nexus IT Recyclers LLP" against "NEXUS IT RECYCLER". Almost always an abbreviation, so a reviewer confirms it in seconds rather than rejecting a real vendor over punctuation.
Different name entirely
A personal account, or a third party's. Rejected. Payouts must go to the registered firm. They can supply a different account or a bank letter.
Bank service unavailable
The penny-drop provider is down. Onboarding continues; the account sits unverified. They can list and sell — they simply cannot be paid until it clears, which we tell them upfront.
Savings account for a company
A registered firm paying into a personal savings account. Allowed for proprietorships, flagged for everyone else.
Changed later, after approval
A verified vendor edits their bank account. Re-verification runs again, payouts freeze for 24 hours, and the owner gets an email and an SMS. Account takeover almost always ends with a changed payout account, so this is the one field we deliberately make slow.
Writes kyc.bank_account kyc.verification_check identity.audit_log
09
Step 7 of 7 · Agreement

Four documents, signed once

The grading policy is the one that matters. It is what we hold them to when a machine reaches our inspection bench and does not match what they declared.

3 minutes

What they sign

  • Vendor agreement — commission, settlement cycle, penalties
  • Grading and inspection policy — what A+, A and B mean, and who pays when they do not match
  • Data wipe undertaking — no customer data leaves with a machine
  • Ownership declaration — every serial they list is legitimately theirs to sell

How the signature is captured

  • Aadhaar e-Sign — preferred, legally strongest
  • Document fingerprint stored — proves the file has not changed since
  • Version recorded — when the policy changes, we know who is on which version
  • IP, time and device recorded
Signed
The application is complete and enters the review queue.
e-Sign fails
Aadhaar OTP does not arrive, or the service is down. We fall back to click-wrap acceptance with the document fingerprint, IP and timestamp. Weaker, but valid, and it does not strand a real vendor.
They want changes to the terms
A large vendor negotiating commission. Routed to the commercial team. Their file waits at this step rather than being rejected.
Terms change later
We revise the grading policy. Every existing vendor must re-accept. We can see exactly who has and who has not, because the version is stored per acceptance.
Writes kyc.agreement_acceptance vendor.vendor_payout_preference customer.org_preference

Part 5 · The decision

10
Internal · Onboarding desk

A named person decides, within 48 working hours

The reviewer sees the submitted data and the government's answer side by side, with a risk score built from everything above. Machines flag; people decide.

48 h SLA

What the reviewer sees

  • Declared versus verified — what they typed next to what the GST portal returned
  • Risk score — age of firm, sourcing channels, verification retries, device velocity
  • Every document — watermarked with the reviewer's own name
  • Duplicate and blocked-list results
  • Trade references — if the volume warrants calling them

The three decisions

  • Approve — status becomes VERIFIED
  • Ask for more — names the specific item, sends them back to that step only
  • Reject — with a reason code, not a shrug

Extra control

  • Two approvers — required above ₹25 lakh expected monthly volume
  • Site visit — for the largest applicants, before the first payout
Approved
Verified, tier set to Bronze, listing rights granted. Welcome pack and a training call are scheduled.
More information needed
One item is wrong. They return to that step alone, not to the beginning. The blocking reason appears at the top of the screen in their own words.
Rejected
A reason code is recorded and shown. They may re-apply after 30 days. The PAN and GSTIN are remembered, so a fresh company name does not reset their history.
SLA about to breach
Nothing decided in 40 hours. Escalates to the onboarding manager. Breaches are reported, not quietly absorbed.
GSTIN cancelled after approval
Our quarterly re-check finds it inactive. Listings pause automatically. Orders already placed are honoured. They are told immediately, with what to do about it.
Writes kyc.kyc_review identity.organization (status) identity.audit_log platform.notification_log

The three ideas underneath all of this

Why onboarding is built this way

1

Never ask twice

Every answer is stored against the organisation, not the form. A vendor adding a second warehouse two years later does not re-enter their GSTIN, and an order never asks for an address they have already given us.

2

Fail loudly and specifically

"Something went wrong" creates a support ticket. "This bill is dated January 2025, please send one from the last three months" creates a correct re-upload. Every red and amber path above names the actual problem.

3

Our outages are never their problem

When the GST portal or the bank service is down, we say so, save the work, queue a retry, and let the application continue. Approval waits for the check. The person does not.
