From a listed laptop to a delivered one

Four journeys, one continuous chain. The big change in this version: inspection now happens at the vendor's premises when stock is listed, not at our hub after an order. That single move removes the mid-order mismatch problem entirely — and creates three new ones, which are named honestly further down.

Part 1 · Getting a technician there

01
Scheduling engine

Matching a technician, a tool seat and a warehouse that is open

Four constraints have to line up, and the third one is the one people forget: our diagnostic tool licence has a fixed number of concurrent seats.

Runs hourly

What has to align

  • A technician covering that zone — and certified on the required tool
  • Enough hours in their day — 40 units at 15 minutes is 10 hours; that is two days, not one
  • A free tool licence seat — we pay for a fixed number of concurrent scans
  • The warehouse is actually open — from the hours and holidays they gave us at onboarding
  • Travel time between stops — maximum three sites a day

How we keep it economic

  • Batch by vendor — several listings from one vendor become one visit
  • Batch by geography — two vendors within 15 km share a route
  • Weight by value — a 200-unit request outranks a 25-unit one
  • Prefer expiring stock — re-inspections that would otherwise kill live listings
Slot found
Technician assigned, vendor notified with a date and a two-hour window. A reminder goes out the evening before.
No technician for 5 days
Demand exceeds field capacity. The vendor is given the real date, not an optimistic one. Ops sees a capacity alert, which is how we know when to hire.
All tool seats in use
Every licence is occupied that day. The visit slips by a day. If this recurs, the licence count is the bottleneck and finance is told, with the number.
Warehouse closed on the proposed day
A regional festival we hold in their holiday calendar. Skipped automatically. This is exactly why we collected holidays at onboarding.
Vendor asks to reschedule
Allowed twice, free. A third reschedule carries the visit fee, because a technician-day held and released is a day we cannot sell.

Part 2 · One machine at a time

03
Per unit · 12 to 18 minutes

The diagnostic tool does the reading

We use a licensed third-party diagnostic product rather than building our own. It boots on the machine, reads the real hardware, and produces a report we ingest. Our job is to interpret it, not to reinvent it.

Automated Third-party tool

What the tool reads off the machine

  • Serial and model from firmware — not from the sticker
  • Processor, cores, memory size and module count
  • Storage type, size, health and hours powered on
  • Battery design capacity, current capacity, cycles
  • Display, keyboard, touchpad, ports, camera, audio
  • Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, thermals under load
  • BIOS password, Secure Boot, TPM, remote-management locks

How we handle it

  • Raw report stored exactly as produced — a disputed reading is re-examined against what the tool actually said
  • Mapped onto our own fields — through a stored field map, so changing tool vendors is configuration
  • Compared against the catalog specification
  • Compared against the vendor's declaration
  • Data wipe run and certified — to a recognised standard
Everything matches
Continue to the cosmetic check.
Serial from firmware differs from the label
The sticker does not belong to this machine. Immediate stop and a fraud flag. This is the single most serious thing a technician can find, and there is no override.
BIOS password, remote lock or tracking software active
The previous owner still controls the machine. Automatic failure. No override exists at any level, because a locked laptop is worthless to a buyer and dangerous to sell.
Specification differs from the catalog
Listed as 16 GB, tool reads 8 GB. The unit is moved to the correct configuration if we have one catalogued, or rejected if we do not. Because no buyer exists yet, this is an edit rather than a dispute.
Tool cannot boot the machine
Locked boot order, dead battery, damaged port. Marked untestable, with a photograph. Untestable is not the same as failed, and the vendor is told which it was.
Report will not parse
The tool changed its output format after an update. The raw file is kept and queued for reprocessing. The technician can enter the key values manually so the visit is not wasted, and the parse failure alerts engineering.
No internet at the warehouse
Common in industrial areas. The app works fully offline. Everything queues locally and syncs when the technician regains signal. Nothing is lost and nothing waits.

Per unit · Cosmetic grading

The part a machine cannot judge

The tool measures function. A scratch on a lid is a human call, and it is the call vendors most often get wrong — usually optimistically rather than dishonestly.

3 minutes

Structured checklist

  • Lid — scratches, dents, paint loss
  • Palm rest and keyboard — wear, shine, missing keycaps
  • Screen surface and bezel — separate from the tool's pixel test
  • Hinges — tight, loose, cracked
  • Base and ports
  • Labels and screws — evidence of opening

Six photographs, fixed angles

  • Lid, palm rest, screen on, base, ports, worst defect *
  • Serial and timestamp burned into each image — not overlaid at display time, so a downloaded photo stays attributable
  • System proposes a grade from the checklist
  • Technician confirms or overrides with a reason — a high override rate means either the checklist or the technician needs work, and we can tell which
Confirms the declared grade
Unit passes as declared. Straight to sealing.
One grade lower than declared
A+ claimed, A confirmed. This is the ordinary case and it is now harmless. The unit is corrected and the vendor is told the same day. No buyer was ever shown the wrong grade.
Two grades lower
A+ claimed, B confirmed. Corrected, and it counts double against grade accuracy. Repeated two-grade errors put a vendor on watchlist.
Worse than grade B
Cracked casing, missing parts, heavy damage. Cannot be listed at all. Grades C and D do not exist on this marketplace, and the technician cannot create them.
Better than declared
A claimed, A+ confirmed — an honest vendor undervaluing stock. Upgraded, with a suggested higher price. It does not count against their accuracy. Rewarding conservative grading is deliberate.

05
Per unit · Sealing

A numbered seal, photographed on the machine

Here is the problem this solves. The laptop passed inspection today, but it sits in the vendor's warehouse until someone buys it — possibly weeks. What stops them swapping it for a worse one?

1 minute The anti-swap control

What happens

  • A tamper-evident seal is applied — across the lid seam
  • Seal code scanned and bound to the serial — permanently paired in our records
  • Photographed in place * — the seal number legible in the frame
  • Unit becomes sellable — the first moment a buyer can see it

What the seal is checked against later

  • At pickup — courier or field executive scans it and photographs it
  • At hub intake — if the shipment routes through a hub
  • At delivery — the buyer sees an intact seal with a number matching their invoice
  • Broken at any point — the unit stops and goes back to inspection
Sealed
The unit is live and sellable. Its seal number appears on the buyer's invoice.
Seal damaged during application
They tear. A replacement is applied and the original number is voided, linked to its replacement. The roll is reconciled at end of day, so a "damaged" seal cannot quietly walk out of the building.
Seal found broken before dispatch
Discovered at pickup, weeks later. The unit cannot ship. It returns to inspection at the vendor's cost. If a buyer had ordered it, they are offered a replacement or a refund immediately, and the vendor bears it.
Vendor needs to open a sealed machine
A legitimate reason — a repair, or a customer demonstration. They request it in the portal. The seal is voided, the unit leaves the marketplace, and it re-enters at the next visit. We would rather lose a sale than ship an unverified machine.

The three problems this model creates — and how each is handled

Inspecting before sale is the right call, but it is not free. These are the costs, stated plainly, so nobody is surprised in month three.

ProblemWhy it is realWhat we do about it
We inspect stock that never sells Under the old hub model we only inspected what was already bought. Now we inspect everything listed. If 40% of listed stock sells, 60% of our inspection cost produces no revenue. A 25-unit minimum per visit, batching by geography, a visit fee below the threshold, and earned sampling for proven vendors. The cost per inspected unit is tracked on every visit, so this is a number we manage rather than a surprise we absorb.
Inspection goes stale Battery health drops and storage wears. A report from six months ago is a claim we can no longer stand behind, and a buyer who finds an 82% battery against a 90% listing is right to be annoyed. Reports expire after 90 days. Units become unsellable automatically. The vendor is warned 14 days ahead so they can reprice and clear the stock rather than lose it.
The machine sits with the vendor Between inspection and sale, the laptop is in their warehouse, not ours. A dishonest vendor could swap the inspected machine for a worse one carrying the same label. A numbered tamper-evident seal, photographed on the machine, verified at every handover, and printed on the buyer's invoice. A broken seal stops the unit. For new and watchlist vendors, shipments route through our hub anyway until they have earned direct dispatch.

Instant

What moving inspection to the vendor's premises actually bought us

Three things got better, one thing got harder

1

The mid-order dispute disappears

Under the hub model, a wrong grade was discovered after a buyer had paid, which meant a paused order, a 24-hour decision window and an unhappy customer. Now it is found before any buyer exists. A correction is an edit, not an incident.

2

Delivery is two to three days faster

Most shipments now go straight from vendor to buyer. No inbound leg, no hub dwell time, no second handling cost. The hub is reserved for broken seals, multi-vendor consolidation, high value and vendors who have not yet earned direct dispatch.

3

Buyers see truth, not claims

Every number on a live listing — grade, battery percentage, storage health, inspection score — was measured on a specific machine by our technician. The certificate is downloadable before they order, not after.

And the harder part: we now pay to inspect stock that may never sell

This is the real cost of the change and it should be on the table in every review. Under the hub model, inspection cost was incurred only on sold units. Now it is incurred on listed units. If a vendor lists 100 and sells 40, we have funded 60 inspections that produced no revenue.

Four controls manage it, and all four are visible in the schema: a 25-unit minimum per visit; geographic batching so one trip serves several vendors; a visit fee below the threshold, waived above it; and earned sampling, where a vendor with thousands of clean inspections behind them has only a proportion of each batch opened. The cost per inspected unit is recorded on every single visit, so this is a number the business steers by rather than one it discovers at year end.
